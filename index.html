Why the signature is where work stalls

Most documents do their travelling before anyone signs them. The contract is negotiated, the form is filled in, the approval is agreed — and then the finished document is emailed as a PDF, printed, signed by hand, scanned, and emailed back. Copies multiply. Versions blur. Someone asks, reasonably, whether the signed copy in the inbox is the final one, and nobody can say for certain.

The chase is its own cost: reminders sent by hand, signers who never saw the request, signed pages filed in inboxes instead of the record. The work was done; the proof is scattered.

Two kinds of signature, explained plainly

An electronic signature is the broad concept: a person indicates their intent to sign a record, consents to sign it electronically, and the act is associated with the record itself. It is about the signer: who they are, and that they meant to sign that document.

A digital signature is a specific technology underneath a signature: it uses a certificate-based digital ID to seal the signed document, so that if anything in the document changes after signing, the seal shows it. It is about the document — proof that what was signed is what is on file, untampered. Both are supported in Outcome Cloud™; the document determines which one the process calls for.

The signing loop, end to end

Stage 1

Prepare

The document is classified, extracted, and validated by MaxxLogix SI™ before anyone is asked to sign — the signer sees the finished document, not a work in progress.

Stage 2

Route

The prepared document goes to the right signer — or to several signers in the right order when a process calls for more than one.

Stage 3

Sign

The signer signs — an electronic signature, or a digital signature where the document calls for a certificate-sealed record.

Stage 4

Validate

Completion is checked. Anything missing, incomplete, or mismatched surfaces as an exception in Outcome Workspace™ instead of failing quietly.

Stage 5

Record

The signed document and its audit trail are stored in the Outcome Repository™ — the signed outcome, on the record, ready to retrieve and report.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an electronic signature and a digital signature?

An electronic signature is the act and the intent: a person consents to sign electronically and their signature is associated with the record. A digital signature is a technology used to protect that record — a certificate-based seal that shows whether the document has changed since it was signed. One is about the signer; the other is about the integrity of the document.

Are electronic signatures legally effective?

Generally, yes. In the United States, the federal ESIGN Act and the state-level Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), adopted in most states, give electronic signatures legal effect for most transactions — a signature may not be denied effect solely because it is electronic. The details of your own documents and obligations are matters for your own legal counsel.

What happens to a document after it is signed?

It is recorded, not just saved. The signed document is stored in the Outcome Repository™ together with its audit trail — who signed, when, and the history of the document through preparation, routing, and validation — so the signed record can be retrieved with its history at any time.

What if a document comes back incomplete or unsigned?

It becomes an exception, not a dead end. Completion checks run when signing finishes; anything missing or mismatched is routed into Outcome Workspace™, where a person sees the document, what is missing, and why it was flagged, and resolves it.

Where do forms and portals fit in?

Forms and portals — handled by MaxxLaunch™ — are how information is submitted: requests and intake captured as structured data from the first click. When the work that follows calls for a signed document, that document runs through the signing loop described here, and the signed result is recorded alongside the submission.
