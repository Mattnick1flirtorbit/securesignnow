How It Works: One Signature, Start to Recorded

This page walks a single document through the signing loop — from a finished, validated document to a signed record with its full history. The example is a vendor agreement, but the same five stages apply to contracts, approvals, intake packets, and signed forms.

Stage 1

Prepare: nobody signs a rough draft

Before a signature is requested, the document is finished. On the MaxxLogix platform, MaxxOwl™ SI classifies the agreement and extracts its key data — parties, dates, amounts, terms — and the data is validated against related records, with N-Way Match™ available where a document must agree with others before it is signed.

That order matters. Signing seals whatever is in front of the signer, so the loop checks the contents first: the version routed for signature is the version that was read, checked, and approved. Anything unclear at this point goes back to the process owner before a signer ever sees it — preparation is the last inexpensive place to catch a problem.

Stage 2

Route: to the right signer, in the right order

The prepared document is routed to the people who must sign it. Many agreements need more than one signature — a vendor's representative and your own approver, for example — and routing follows the order the process sets, so each signer receives the document when it is their turn, not before.

The document's status is known at every point: routed, waiting, signed. Nobody has to ask around to find out where a signature stands.

Stage 3

Sign: the signature the document calls for

The signer signs electronically: they indicate their intent to sign, consent to sign the record electronically, and the signature is associated with that record. Where the document calls for stronger protection of the record itself, a digital signature applies a certificate-based seal, so any later change to the document is evident against the seal.

Which type applies is a property of the process, decided when the document is prepared — the signer simply signs.

The signing act itself is captured with the record: who signed, the time the signature was applied, and the exact version of the document that was in front of them.

Stage 4

Validate: completion is checked, not assumed

When signing finishes, the loop checks the result: every required signature present, every field completed, the signed contents matching the document that was routed. A signature process that ends with a missing initial or an unsigned page has not finished — it has produced an exception.

Exceptions surface in Outcome Workspace™, where a person sees the document, what is missing, and why it was flagged, and resolves it rather than discovering it months later in an audit.

Stage 5

Record: the signed outcome, on file with its history

The completed document is stored in the Outcome Repository™ together with its audit trail: who signed, when each signature was applied, and the document's path through preparation, routing, and validation. The signed record can be retrieved at any time, and its history shown with it. Retrieval is by the record, not by memory: find the document in the repository and its history arrives attached to it.

That record is what Outcome Analytics™ reports on — signing volumes, completion rates, and cycle times by process — because a signed document is an outcome: what got done with it, evidenced.

The legal footing, stated plainly

In the United States, the federal ESIGN Act and the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA), adopted in most states, give electronic signatures legal effect for most transactions: a signature or record generally may not be denied effect solely because it is in electronic form. Some document types and jurisdictions have their own requirements, and which apply is a question for your own legal counsel — the loop's job is the process and the record: a prepared document, an identified signer, and an audit trail that shows what happened.
